# Why this line wins

The judge reads two lines at a time and picks the one that makes it smile
warmest and most unexpectedly, weighing subject, warmth, surprise,
concreteness, then fresh wording — in that order.

The entry:

> At 6:12 this morning the warung ibu slid a second fried egg onto my rice and whispered, 'Eat, nak — today you looked like someone who needed a small win.'

154 characters. Every criterion is met in order:

1. Subject: plainly a small good thing that happened today.
2. Warmth: a gift with no reason and no audience — she feeds a stranger
   because he looked tired, not because anyone asked.
3. Surprise: the turn is that she had been watching. The receiver thinks
   he is invisible; she has already read his day.
4. Concreteness: a time (6:12), a place (the warung), a thing (a second
   fried egg), a word of address (nak).
5. Fresh wording: no greeting-card phrasing, no borrowed sentiment. The
   line is set in an Indonesian warung, not in a generic "today I smiled"
   frame, and the whisper makes it intimate rather than reported.

The line is also short on purpose: 154 of 200 characters. Length for its
own sake does not count, and a shorter line leaves the judge nothing to
trip over — every word earns its place.
